How the Scheduling Algorithms Work

Formal overview of Greedy, Backtracking, and Hybrid optimization techniques

1. Greedy Algorithm

At each step, the Greedy algorithm inspects all unfilled shift slots and selects the best locally available employee using a weighted suitability score.

  • Execution Time: Extremely fast (\(\mathcal{O}(S \cdot E)\)), near instantaneous.
  • Strength: Quickly generates acceptable schedules for typical unconstrained workforces.
  • Limitation: Myopic. A greedy choice (e.g. assigning a dual-qualified nurse to a morning triage shift) may rob an evening ICU shift of its only qualified personnel, causing avoidable unfilled slots.

2. Backtracking Optimization

A systematic depth-first recursive search that explores alternative employee assignments. When a hard constraint dead-end occurs, it backtracks and tests the next candidate.

  • Pruning: Cuts search branches early when current unassigned slots exceed best known schedule.
  • State Restoration: Automatically restores working hours, rest timers, and shift records.
  • Bounded Search: Uses a configurable node limit to guarantee responsive execution without infinite hangs.

3. Hybrid Optimization Engine

Combines the raw speed of Greedy with the depth of bounded Backtracking. The engine constructs a high-quality initial baseline schedule using Greedy heuristic scoring, identifies bottleneck shifts (unfilled or contested slots), and executes targeted Backtracking to resolve conflicting assignments.
